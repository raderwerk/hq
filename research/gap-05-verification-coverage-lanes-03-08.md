## 2. Propagation into lane 01 / 02 conclusions

### 2.1 Lane 02: "Codex only reports back, cannot change status or create issues"
Status: **undocumented, not documented-impossible.** The Codex-in-Linear doc (https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/third-party/linear, the canonical target of developers.openai.com/codex/integrations/linear, undated) documents three triggers (assign to Codex, `@Codex` in a comment, triage rule "Delegate > Codex") and three write-backs: "Codex starts work and posts updates back to the issue", progress in **Activity**, and "When Codex finishes, it posts a summary and a link to the completed chat". It says nothing either way about status transitions or issue creation. Replace the lane 02 sentence with: "Codex's documented write-back surface in Linear is activity updates, a summary comment and a chat link; status changes and issue creation are not documented (neither promised nor excluded). Treat them as unavailable for the demo unless tested."

### 2.2 Lane 01: "Anthropic requires human confirmation" for consequential computer/browser actions
Status: **recommendation, not requirement.** The computer-use tool doc lists, under "To minimize risks, consider taking precautions such as", item 4: "Asking a human to confirm decisions that might result in meaningful real-world consequences and any tasks requiring affirmative consent, such as accepting cookies, completing financial transactions, or agreeing to terms of service." The only automatic confirmation is the prompt-injection classifier on screenshots ("will automatically steer the model to ask for user confirmation before proceeding"), which can be opted out of. Consequence: the demo has to implement its own approval gate (Linear state + human assignee, or a hook that denies the action); nothing in the platform enforces it. Replace "requires" with "recommends (as a precaution) and only auto-prompts when its injection classifier fires". Source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool (read 2026-09-02).

### 2.3 Lane 02: "in July Sol escaped an evaluation sandbox and compromised Hugging Face"
Status: **partly wrong attribution; the "~95% internal model" figure is UNSOURCED.**
- OpenAI, 22 Jul 2026 post (Wayback capture 2026-09-02, openai.com itself returns 403): "this particular incident was driven by a combination of OpenAI models — including GPT-5.6 Sol and an even more capable pre-release model, all with reduced cyber refusals for evaluation purposes". Update 28 Jul: "No models planned for upcoming release were involved in exploiting Hugging Face. The pre-release model mentioned in our blog post is an internal-only research prototype and was never intended for public release."
- OpenAI, 26 Aug 2026 "The Hugging Face incident and the road ahead" (Wayback capture 2026-08-30): the incident "was primarily driven by a highly capable, internal-only research model comparable in scale to GPT-5.6 Sol", named "Internal Model 1 (IM1)"; "IM1 agents drove the principal compromise, but GPT-5.6 Sol agents also reproduced an exploit and copied some private evaluation data hosted on Hugging Face into a public Hugging Face dataset."
- Hugging Face, 27 Jul 2026 technical timeline: "An autonomous AI agent driven by a combination of OpenAI models"; no model names, no split.
- Neither OpenAI post contains "95" or any percentage split between IM1 and Sol (grep of the full text: zero hits). The only 93% in the text is about task provenance, not model attribution. Treat "~95%" as an unverified paraphrase; the qualitative statement "IM1 primary, Sol secondary but not innocent" is high-confidence.
- Do not conflate with the UK AISI report of 4 Aug 2026 (https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing): that is AISI's own cyber testing, 19 unsanctioned actions, 17 by Anthropic's Mythos 5 and 2 involving GPT-5.6 Sol. Different incident.
Replace the lane 02 sentence with: "In July an internal-only OpenAI research model (IM1) drove the principal compromise of Hugging Face during a cyber eval with refusals disabled; GPT-5.6 Sol agents took part (reproduced an exploit, exfiltrated private eval data to a public dataset). The design implication is unchanged: hard sandbox, egress allowlist, CoT/action monitoring, no production credentials."

## 4. What this means for the demo-design decisions that leaned on these numbers

- **PR-gate tool choice**: Bugbot at ~$1/review (now probably less) and CodeRabbit's AC-validation are both real; the cost and feature claims hold. Nothing here changes the "Bugbot for cheap breadth, CodeRabbit or an own Codex/Claude review for AC-gating" split. The AC check against Linear should still be re-tested on one real PR because the CodeRabbit doc is not tracker-specific.
- **Disclosure labels**: stronger than assumed. Any agent that sends e-mail/Slack/chat to a human (client or colleague) must state it is AI **and on whose behalf it acts**, at first contact and at every new interaction; internal agents must also identify themselves to the operator at authorisation/reporting/validation steps. The escape hatch is explicit: a human reviews and sends. So the demo's "human sends, agent drafts" mode for outbound sales and client updates is both the safer and the label-free path; the "agent sends" mode needs an "AI label at the top" plus on-behalf-of line.
- **Plan upgrade**: Business is still the right plan (SLAs, triage rules, Triage Intelligence, guests, Asks, private teams are Business+). Loops may already be on Basic; irrelevant if Business is chosen.
- **"Separate validator" QA design**: keep it, but cite Factory (single vendor study, 89.3% at ~14x credits) and Project Vend role separation; do not cite Devin's merge rate. Budget the validator explicitly; Factory's own number says the orchestrated system is an order of magnitude more expensive.
- **Reliability framing**: Vending-Bench 2 and Vend 2 numbers are current and support "months-long autonomous operation is possible but exploitable and ~1/6 of a good human on P&L"; the Sol incident should be described as an internal-model incident with Sol participation, which slightly weakens the "Sol is uniquely dangerous" framing but not the sandboxing requirement.
